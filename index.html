An atlas of Italian culture · Information Visualization · University of Bologna, 2023/24

Where you live in Italy decides how much culture you can reach.

Italy has more than thirteen thousand libraries and four and a half thousand museums, but they are not spread, used or priced evenly. This atlas follows public data from ISTAT and the Ministry of Culture region by region, adjusts for population wherever the data allows, and lets you open every institution on the map one by one.

By Corrado Consiglio, Salvatore Di Marzo and Alice Picco

Chapter ISupply

Counting libraries rewards big provinces. Per inhabitant, the map changes.

A map of raw counts mostly shows where people live: Rome has the most libraries simply because it has the most residents. ISTAT's 2012 table gives both the count and the rate per 10,000 inhabitants for every province. Scroll on and the same map redraws from one measure to the other.

Chapter IIThe atlas

Every library, museum and archive we could place on a map.

This map joins two public registers: the Ministry of Culture's "Luoghi della cultura" (museums, archives, state libraries, monuments and archaeological sites, with address, opening hours and full ticket price) and the ICCU register of libraries (name and position). Search for a place, filter by type or free entry, and open any dot to read its record.

      Chapter IIIReach

      One municipality in four has no library of its own.

      We placed each of the 13,319 geolocated libraries in the 2022 register inside the municipal boundary that contains it. 2,040 of the 7,899 municipalities in the boundary file contain none. This gap does not follow the north–south line: Abruzzo and Piemonte have the highest shares, Sardegna and Sicilia some of the lowest.

      Chapter IVUse

      Southern libraries lend a fraction of what northern ones do.

      Having a library is not the same as using one. ISTAT's 2022 survey reports the average number of local loans per library that answered, which already removes the effect of how many libraries a region has. We use public libraries for the ranking: in Lazio a few private libraries account for 5.5 million loans, which would push its all-library average to 18,218.

      Chapter VReading

      Where libraries lend less, fewer people read.

      The reading survey is already a share of the population (people aged 6 and over, reading outside school and work), so regions can be compared directly. The two panels share one order: regions ranked by the share who read at least one book.

      Chapter VIPrice

      Price is not where the gap is.

      Of the 4,559 museums in the Ministry's open register, 2,556 state their entry conditions: 1,370 free and 1,186 with a ticket. Free entry does not follow the north–south line.

      Chapter VIIIFindings

      What the data says

      1. Use is the widest gap.

        Libraries are only somewhat scarcer in the south (1.8 against 2.3 per 10,000), but they lend 17 times less. The gap in use is far wider than the gap in supply, so more buildings alone would not close it. See loans per library

      2. Small places are left out, north and south.

        One municipality in four has no library; the share is highest in Abruzzo (56%), Piemonte (42%) and Liguria (41%). Rural libraries lend 9 times less than urban ones. See reach

      3. Reading follows the same line.

        The four largest southern regions have the fewest readers (27–29%), and the regions where libraries lend least read least. See reading and loans

      4. Counts mislead; rates help.

        Absolute counts crown Rome and Milan. Per inhabitant, Trieste, the Alpine provinces and parts of Sardinia lead, and the densest commuter provinces fall behind. See the supply map

      Chapter IXMethod & sources

      How the numbers were made

      Data
      ISTAT tables on libraries (2010–2022), library loans (2019–2022) and reading habits (2011–2021); the ICCU register of libraries; the Ministry of Culture's open "Luoghi della cultura" dataset on dati.cultura.gov.it (museums, archives, libraries, heritage sites, entry prices). All files are in the repository.
      Per capita
      Libraries per 10,000 inhabitants are ISTAT's own rates for 2012. The repository has no population figures for other years, so we did not compute rates ourselves. Loans are ISTAT's average per library; reading figures are already shares of the population.
      Headline figures
      68× = 10,137 ÷ 149 (loans per public library, Emilia-Romagna ÷ Campania, 2022). 17× = 7,631 ÷ 441 (north-east ÷ south). 9× = 12,655 ÷ 1,429 (cities ÷ rural areas). One in four = 2,040 ÷ 7,899 municipalities.
      The atlas
      Each record card shows the fields the source provides: name, type, address and postcode, town, province and region as recorded, website, opening hours (in Italian, as recorded), the full ticket price, a short excerpt of the description, the record's own source and last update. The export also lists a reduced price, but it equals the full price in all 1,326 records, so it is not shown. Phone numbers and e-mails are left out. ICCU libraries carry only a name and a position. The municipality line is computed from the coordinates. Zoomed out, points are counted into hexagons about 20 pixels across.
      Your place
      Ranks use the same figures as the charts: 20 regions, or the provinces that have a value. Counts of places are computed from the atlas and are counts, not rates.
      Areas
      North: Piemonte, Valle d'Aosta, Lombardia, Trentino-Alto Adige, Veneto, Friuli-Venezia Giulia, Liguria, Emilia-Romagna. Centre: Toscana, Umbria, Marche, Lazio. South & islands: Abruzzo, Molise, Campania, Puglia, Basilicata, Calabria, Sicilia, Sardegna (ISTAT's "Mezzogiorno").
      Processing
      Data gathering and cleaning in Python notebooks (in data/). The figures on this page are rebuilt from those files by scripts/build_story_data.py, which also simplifies the province boundaries, runs the point-in-polygon counts and extracts the atlas records from the RDF export.
      Colour
      Terracotta, library green and ink blue mark libraries, museums and archives; they were checked for colour-blind separation on both themes. Every mark also has its own shape, and every chart has a data table.
